Project overview

Project context & objective

This co-authored 2020 research project investigated the use of an Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS) to predict electricity consumption. The publicly accessible paper considers historic consumption and weather-related variables when forecasting energy demand.

Energy-demand forecasts require organised inputs and clear evaluation of model behaviour. The study examined a neuro-fuzzy approach to consumption forecasting.

Electricity-demand prediction supports power-system planning and operations. Consumption depends on multiple conditions, including weather, and historical observations may not always be complete or reliable. The paper examines an adaptive approach to forecasting in that context.

Research publication

Prediction of Electricity Consumption using Adaptive Neuro-fuzzy Inference System (2020).

Authors: Sai Vikas Malladi, Sardar Maran P., and Venkat Sankalp Mandava.

The research explores electricity consumption forecasting using an adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS), considering historical consumption and weather-related inputs. The available SSRN record identifies this as a publicly posted research paper; it does not independently establish peer-reviewed journal publication.

Methodology

From inputs to insights

The following stages explain the workflow and the technical decisions behind this project.

01

Research framing

The paper identifies electricity load and consumption forecasting as important for planning. It considers the challenges of forecasting from historical information and weather conditions.

02

Input considerations

The abstract describes electricity-consumption records and weather data available online as sources for model assessment.

03

ANFIS methodology

The proposed approach uses an adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system to predict future consumption from previous observations and weather parameters.

04

Published research record

The work was co-authored by Sai Vikas Malladi, Sardar Maran P. and Venkat Sankalp Mandava. SSRN records the paper as written in June 2020, posted in July 2020 and revised in August 2020.
